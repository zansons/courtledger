About our numbers

CourtLedger is built around two player rankings: AP (Added Points) first and DPM (DARKO) second. Everything else on the site (player dashboards, stats and scatter plots, trends, head-to-head, teams, game logs, lineups) is under More in the menu.

AP (Added Points)

AP (Added Points) is our Dean Oliver-based measure of how many points a player added to, or cost, his team in a single game, compared with an average player. It is not plus-minus or net rating: it credits each player for what he actually did on each play, not just for being on the floor.

  • Built from play-by-play. Following Dean Oliver's Net Points approach (Basketball Beyond Paper), every shot, turnover, rebound and free throw is valued, and the credit or blame for it is divided among the ten players on the floor at that moment.
  • O-AP (offense) shares the credit for every made shot, and the blame for every miss or turnover, among all five players on offense, the way Dean Oliver divides it: the harder a player's part of the play, the more credit he gets when it works, and the easier it was, the more blame he takes when it fails. Shot difficulty comes from where the shot was taken, and teammates who space the floor get a share too. O-AP then compares what each player produced with what an average player would produce with the same possessions.
  • D-AP (defense) shares every defensive play among the five defenders on the floor, then refines it with tracked individual defense, meaning the points a player actually allowed on the possessions he defended. The more possessions he was tracked on, the more that tracking counts.
  • It always adds up. In every game, a team's Added Points add up to its final margin, adjusted for any difference in the number of possessions the two teams had.

The AP ranking shows totals, per game, per 40 minutes, and per 100 team possessions on court. AP for a game is published once individual defensive data for that game is loaded. provisional defense marks any game where it isn't, so defense there is shared by playing time only.

DPM (DARKO)

DPM (DARKO) is our EuroLeague version of DARKO, the Daily Plus-Minus model created by Kostya Medvedovsky (darko.app) for the NBA. It isn't a model we invented; we apply the DARKO approach to EuroLeague data. It is a forward-looking rating: how many points per 100 possessions a player is expected to add over an average EuroLeague player in his next game. It weighs every EuroLeague game since 2007-08, with recent games counting most, and blends box-score production with lineup results. O-DPM and D-DPM split it into offense and defense.

DPM is a current rating. In the DPM ranking, the season switch picks which season's players are listed and their games and minutes; the rating shown is always the latest one.

Freshness

Both rankings are rebuilt after every EuroLeague game day.

DPM ratings are current through the latest game in the DPM column. AP appears for a game once its defensive data is loaded, so it can trail DPM by a few games.

Changelog

2 Nov 2026 (update). Offensive credit and blame now follow Dean Oliver's own rules for dividing a play among teammates: shot difficulty and every teammate on the floor count, not just the shooter and passer. Rebounds are shared the same way. Defense is unchanged. Every game still adds up exactly to the final margin. AP values from before this update are not comparable.

2 Nov 2026. AP is now calculated from play-by-play for 2025-26 and 2026-27, following Dean Oliver's Net Points method: credit and blame are divided on every play, then compared with an average player per possession. AP values from before this date are not comparable.

30 Oct 2026. AP was recalculated for 2025-26 and 2026-27 with a refined Dean Oliver-based method. Offense and defense now follow Oliver's box-score framework more closely, and tracked individual defense counts more for players tracked on more possessions. Every game still adds up exactly to the final margin. AP values from before this date are not comparable.
